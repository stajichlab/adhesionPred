**Kerr 2016: facts from the full text.**

1. **No epithelial cells were tested.** The assays are mucin binding (chamber slides, conidia), FleA binding to macrophages (FACS, recombinant protein), and phagocytosis (RAW264.7, human alveolar macrophages). The abstract's "macrophages bind and phagocytose" refers to an internalisation count: "Internalized conidia were counted as conidia within the boundary of the cell that were not stained with calcofluor white." So the 40 to 50% figure is **internalisation**, not adhesion.
2. **Conidia were fixed.** Phagocytosis and mucin assays used "PFA-fixed WT or ΔfleA" conidia. Sakai 2019 counted adhered conidia as colonies after an agar overlay, which needs living conidia (my inference; the fetched text does not use the word "live"). The two papers therefore did not run the same assay.
3. **Numbers:** "the internalization of ΔfleA conidia was 50% less than that of WT" (RAW264.7) and "a reduction in phagocytosis of 40%" (human alveolar macrophages). Mucin binding of ΔfleA: "much weaker"; no value in the text. 6 replicates per condition (3 independent experiments or 3 donors).
4. **No complemented strain.** "Despite repeated attempts to complement the ΔfleA deletion strains, we encountered problems such as phenotypic abnormalities in spore size and shape, which precluded generation of reliable complement reagents." Three independent deletion strains were used, and a glycomimetic inhibitor (2EHex) gave the same result for mucin binding and phagocytosis of wild-type conidia.
5. **Localisation:** FleA-RFP fluorescence was "high on resting conidia", low in swollen conidia, "largely absent in hyphae". FleA "lacks a canonical secretion signal peptide according to the Signal P 4.0 server"; "resting conidia shed significant amounts of FleA" (Western blot of 10x concentrated supernatant).
6. **Mouse:** immunocompetent C57BL/6, intranasal 5x10^7 conidia, harvest at day 3; n = 5 (germination counts, BAL cells) or 10 (haemoglobin, 18S RNA). ΔfleA gave more germination, higher lavage haemoglobin and higher fungal 18S signal. The paper also made ΔfleA *A. flavus* (mucin binding "much weaker").
7. **Phylogeny (S1 Fig):** "FleA is present in the genomes of only a few Aspergillus species ... FleA is also present in other human pathogens including dermatophytes (Trichophyton, Microsporum and Arthroderma)". Method (S1 Fig legend): *Aleuria aurantia* lectin used to BLAST NCBI; a conserved region was extracted for a FastTree tree.
8. Authors' own wording: "FleA is not a virulence factor"; "Our experiments prove that FleA is a pathogen-associated molecular pattern"; "It is unclear why A. fumigatus and other pathogenic fungi have evolved to express fleA".

## 3. Answers to the specific questions

### 3.1 Sakai 2019 and Kerr 2016 on FleA (items 1, 2, 5)

1. Sakai 2019: adhesion of ΔfleA conidia to A549, J774 and MH-S **did not differ** from the parent. Phagocytosis rate and 8-h survival also did not differ. No effect sizes can be given (not in the text; Fig 2C only). The direction rests on a summarising-tool fetch of the journal page. The PDF is not reachable.
2. Kerr 2016 measured **internalisation** of fixed conidia by RAW264.7 and human alveolar macrophages (40 to 50% lower for ΔfleA), and conidia binding to **mucin**. It tested **no epithelial cells** and no live-conidia adhesion to cultured cells. So Sakai and Kerr do not test the same readout. The statement in spec F14 that the two papers "disagree on adhesion" should read: Kerr reports lower internalisation by macrophages of fixed ΔfleA conidia; Sakai reports no difference in adhesion or phagocytosis of live conidia by cultured cells. Only the phagocytosis readouts overlap, and the conidia treatment and cell lines differ.
3. Both knockout papers agree that ΔfleA conidia cause equal or worse disease. Neither shows a loss of epithelial attachment. The only conidia-binding loss with a knockout is binding to purified airway mucin (Kerr, strains ×3, plus an inhibitor).
4. Richard 2018 adds that exogenous FleA does not change conidial adhesion to BEAS-2B (about 98% with or without FleA) but blocks an epithelial antifungal effect on germination. This is a host-recognition effect, not attachment.
5. Houser 2013 shows host-glycan binding of recombinant FleA. Its "conidia attachment" sentence is a belief. The surface-location data are antibody staining after permeabilisation.
6. FleA has no signal peptide and is shed from resting conidia (Kerr, Western blot).
7. FleA orthologs were reported in *Trichophyton*, *Microsporum* and *Arthroderma* (Kerr S1 Fig, BLAST with AAL). These are three of the six genera of the pool S Onygenales PF07938 proteins. No paper tested a dermatophyte protein of this family.

### 3.2 Paracoccin: 70 kDa versus 218 aa, and where GlcNAc binding sits

- UniProt C1G842 has 218 aa (spec F20), about 24 kDa unglycosylated.
- Alegre 2014 reports the recombinant full-length product at **28 kDa** and says that the activities of the native preparation "could be attributed to the 28-kDa conserved hypothetical protein". Fernandes 2017 reports a **27-kDa** Western band with anti-PCN antibody. So later papers put the PADG_03347 product at 27 to 28 kDa, close to 218 aa plus glycosylation.
- The **70 kDa** value is from the older preparation (Coltri 2006; dos Reis Almeida 2010, 2-D blot Mr 70,000). Alegre 2014 calls the link between that band and the protein an assumption that "is now tested" and re-assigns the activity to the 28-kDa product. Coltri 2006 reported that the mass spectra "did not match any known protein"; Alegre 2014 reports that the proteomic analysis of a later preparation matched PADG_03347. Why the native band ran at 70 kDa (glycosylation, a complex, a different protein) is **not answered** by any paper I read.
- **GlcNAc binding and GH18.** No paper maps the GlcNAc-binding or laminin-binding site by mutation or truncation. The four sites are sequence predictions (Clustal Omega comparison; Prosite) and lie inside residues 29-218, which is the GH18 range. The 3D model uses a GH18 chitinase template. The one experiment that bears on the question is the truncation of Alegre 2014: the largest exon (22 kDa predicted, most of the protein) still bound laminin but had only residual NAGase activity. This suggests that binding does not need full NAGase activity. It does not separate a lectin fold from GH18 substrate binding. Conclusion for D9 stands: a paracoccin model would be a GH18 model.
- The virulence effect of overexpression goes through the chitinase site (Pitangui 2021, Gonçales 2021 abstract). The silencing result is a knockdown, and adherence to A549 and phagocytosis did not change (Fernandes 2017).

### 3.3 Pitangui 2021 silencing statement: primary source

The statement is from **Fernandes 2017**, mBio 8(4), PMID 28720727, DOI 10.1128/mBio.00537-17 (full text read). Antisense knockdown (40 to 60% of wild-type expression). Adherence to A549 (2 h CFU) and phagocytosis by peritoneal macrophages (4 h) did not differ. The earlier PubMed search "paracoccin silencing adherence Paracoccidioides" gave 0 hits because the PubMed word-mapping did not match; the title is "Impact of Paracoccin Gene Silencing on Paracoccidioides brasiliensis Virulence".
