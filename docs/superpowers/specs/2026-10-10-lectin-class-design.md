## 1. The question

Which fungal proteins should the tool call "lectin" in the host-interaction tier, with which models, which truth, and which status? The answer in short:

1. Revision 2 proposed a call defined by one Pfam model, Fungal_lectin PF07938. Review 2 showed that this fails the spec's own rule when ubiquity is measured on the population that the call reads: PF07938 is in 392 of 653 genera (60%) on whole proteomes. Section 3.1 gives three options for the owner: (a) a gated call only, (b) a custom score cutoff, (c) no lectin call in v1 with all lectin-like models reported only. My recommendation is (c). It rests on reasoning, not on performance data. No performance data exists for any option.
2. The truth set is `smoke` at most. Section 4.4 gives the cluster counts. No species reaches 20 independent positive clusters. In most species the status stays `unvalidated`. Under option (c) there is no call to measure.
3. The truth is partly circular for a family-defined class. Section 4.1 says how the spec limits this.
4. A published GlcNAc-binding, laminin-binding protein exists in Onygenales: paracoccin of *Paracoccidioides* (F20). The Pfam lectin models do not detect it. Its only Pfam hit is a GH18 chitinase domain, and its predicted lectin sites lie inside that domain. I found no fungal-lectin paper for *Coccidioides*, *Histoplasma* or *Blastomyces* (F17, not found by these searches). The discovery-pass families in Onygenales are associations, not functions.
5. The role of FleA is disputed in the literature (F14). The spec does not decide it.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Which models define the class (task item 1)

**Scope of the model list (review 2, M1).** The Pfam models in this section were chosen by a name search for lectin, agglutinin, galectin or glycan binding (F4). Carbohydrate-binding proteins whose model has no such word are not in the list. Avr4 of *C. fulvum* is one (CBM14; F24). I checked Avr4 and the two chitin-binding models (F24). I did not make a full list of carbohydrate-binding modules (CBM families, WSC). The spec decides Avr4 and CBM14 below.

**M1** means the set of models in a lectin call; M2 is reported only; M3 is excluded. **Rule (one rule for every model).** Two inputs are stated for every model. Both come from the facts table.

- **H, host-interaction evidence from a paper** for a fungal member of the model's family. **H1a**: the paper shows binding to a host glycan or host cell, or an immune effect that depends on host-glycan binding (FleA, SapL1). **H1b**: the paper shows binding to the fungus's own chitin that hides it from host immunity or protects it from host chitinases (Ecp6, Avr4). The two are different mechanisms. **H2**: the paper shows a virulence or infection-stage phenotype or a localisation, but not the host target or the mechanism. **H0**: no such paper found by my searches. H0 is not proof of absence.
- **U, ubiquity**: the share of the 653 genera with at least one protein of the family **in the population that the call reads**. For an ungated call this is the whole proteome. For a call gated on a signal peptide this is pool S (F5). I call U "rare" below 10% and "common" at 10% or above. **The 10% threshold is my choice. No data supports it.** The owner decides it (D2). (Revision 2 measured U on pool S for an ungated call. That was an error; see F25.)

A model joins a call if H is H1a or H1b, U is rare, and the model is not owned by an active narrow-tier family and not a house-keeping class. Models that do not join go to a reported-only set **M2** or an exclusion set **M3**. The cutoff is the Pfam GA (F7) unless option (b) below is chosen.

**Two caveats on H.** (1) H1 needs a paper. Families from lung pathogens and model plant pathogens get papers; mushroom families and rare Ascomycota families get fewer. So H partly measures how much a family has been studied, not only what it does. The rule favours families with a famous member. This is a bias of the rule, not a finding. (2) H0 and H2 rows are not shown to be inert.

**Whole-proteome U (measured, F25).** PF07938 is in 392 of 653 genera (60.0%). Under the rule, PF07938 is **common** for an ungated call. LysM (78% on pool S, so at least 78%) is common. Only these two models have H1 (H1a for PF07938, H1b for LysM). **So the ungated M1 is empty.** The revision 2 conclusion "PF07938 is rare (4%)" described 32 of the 997 PF07938 proteins, the ones with a signal peptide.

## 4. Truth set (task item 3)

### 4.1 Circularity and tiers

The class is defined by Pfam models. Three problems follow:

1. **A positive that is "found by the model" is not evidence.** The rule of the task is kept: a protein is not truth because a Pfam model hits it.
2. **UniLectin3D classes and Pfam models share sequences.** Pfam seeds may contain the same structure sequences (not checked; there is no `Pfam-A.seed` in the Pfam 38.2 directory). PF07938 has 3 seed sequences (F7). Leakage for all structure-derived rows is `in_reference` until checked.
3. **A recall measured on structure-defined proteins measures how well the model covers the structure it was built from.** It is not recall on unknown proteins. The report says this beside every number.

Tiers (follow the hydrophobin tiers):

| Tier | Evidence | Use |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | A paper reports that the protein **binds a glycan** (structure with ligand, ITC, glycan array or inhibition by a sugar) **and** the sequence is a fungal protein with a UniProt accession. The PMID is recorded. | primary positives |
| T2 | UniLectin3D curated lectin structure without a recorded ligand, or a Swiss-Prot reviewed entry with experimental sugar-binding annotation (ECO:0000269) | primary positives |
| T3 | Family or name by sequence similarity only | secondary, reported apart, never primary |
| T4 | Review judgement from sequence, domain and architecture evidence without a paper | reported only, never truth, never used for sensitivity |
| H flag | A paper reports host-glycan or host-cell binding or a host-relevant phenotype (H1 or H2 of section 3.1). Example: FleA (PMIDs 24340081, 27058347, 29370403), SapL1 (34373510). ## 5. Curation workflow (task item 4)

Pattern: `analysis/hydrophobin_truth/curation_rubric.md` (written before any decision; evidence lines; decision rules in order; outcomes `lectin (T4)`, `not lectin (T4)`, `unresolved`; T4 never in truth; the owner can overrule).

### 5.1 Evidence lines (written before any decision)

| Line | Meaning | Independent of the Pfam call? |
|---|---|---|
| A | A 3D structure with a glycan ligand exists for the protein or for an ortholog at 90% identity or more over 90% of length (UniLectin3D or PDB) | yes |
| B | Hit to an M1 model at GA | no |
| C | A paper reports **glycan binding** for this gene product (PMID, quote) | yes |
| C-H | A paper reports host binding or a host-relevant phenotype without glycan binding. It sets the H flag. It does not make a lectin call. | yes |
| D | Reciprocal best hit at 40% identity and 70% coverage of both to a T1 or T2 protein | partly |

## 9. Risks

1. **Truth is structure-derived and mostly from mushrooms.** A recall number says little about pathogen lectins. Stated in the report.
2. **Circularity with Pfam.** Leakage `in_reference`; status capped at `smoke`.
3. **Gate.** SignalP 6 agrees with UniProt and SignalP 4.0 (F12, F26): the AAL-like lectins with host evidence are not in the secretome pools of the discovery pass. The discovery enrichments for PF07938 describe the small subset with a signal peptide.
4. **Pfam release mismatch.** Pools use Pfam-A 37.x, GA values 38.2 (F6).
5. **UniLectin3D enumeration is a lower bound** (197 of 202, F9). One accession has no sequence. Counts can change.
6. **The mislabel** of *Ralstonia* (F9) shows that the origin field needs a check per row.
7. **Pool U is 96.8% of pool S** (F5): "no mechanism" is a weak filter, so pool U is not a test of whether a lectin family is unexplained.
8. **Role over-reading.** A lectin domain does not show that the protein binds a host glycan or triggers inflammation. Lebreton 2021 states that its predictions are not experimentally validated (F16).
9. **A Pfam-defined class misses lectins that Pfam does not model.** Paracoccin is one (F20). The report lists this gap; it does not close it.
10. **The M1/M2 rule is untested, and H partly measures research attention.** The ubiquity threshold (10%) and the H levels are my choices. The threshold is not the deciding input: any threshold between 4.3% and 60% changes the PF07938 result when U is measured on whole proteomes (review 2). H1 needs a paper, so families from lung pathogens and model plant pathogens are favoured, and mushroom and rare Ascomycota families are not. H1a (host-glycan binding) and H1b (masking own chitin) are different mechanisms under one level.
11. **The model list comes from a name search** (F4). Carbohydrate-binding effectors with other names (Avr4, CBM14) are outside it (F24, D11).
12. **SignalP 6 was run in fast mode on UniProt sequences** (F26). It agrees with UniProt and with SignalP 4.0 for FleA. The gate result for the 32 pool S proteins is not checked against their FleA relationship.
